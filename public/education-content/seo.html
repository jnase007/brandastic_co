Their content / SEO system (exact)

This page is not my opinion. It is what they said they do. We copy this system for Brandastic services.

Their rule in one sentence: For every service, sub-service, or category, publish 8 pieces of content. Do that across the catalog until you dominate the keywords.

1. Map the catalog first (they start here)

They organize the business into three levels:

LevelWhat they called itTheir example
Category (macro / industry)Industry-related bucketAdvertising for B2B
ServiceMain offerPaid media · SEO · (and other core services)
Sub-service (micro)Specialty under a serviceABM under paid media

Brandastic first job: write down every service we sell, every sub-service under it, and the industry/category angles we care about. That list is the content map. Nothing gets written until the map exists.

2. For EACH item on that map: exactly 8 pieces

They were explicit. Per service or sub-service or category:

#TypeTheir example (paid media / B2B)
1Ultimate guide (educational asset)“Ultimate guide to paid media for B2B SaaS”
2Listicle (best-of / ranking)“Top 27 paid media agencies for B2B”
3Buyer guide (how to hire / what to look for)“What to look for when hiring a paid media agency as a B2B company”
4–8Five thought-leadership piecese.g. “13 tactics that take your ABM campaigns to the next level”

They grouped 1–3 as guides / listicles / long-form, then five thought-leadership posts = 8 total.

The ultimate guide is the big educational asset (this is the “PDF or big asset” idea — a serious guide people use, not a thin blog).

3. How the website is structured (their technical setup)

  1. Create service subfolders on the site (example path style: content lives under service folders).
  2. Put the supporting pages in those folders.
  3. Some of those pages are not in the main menu on purpose — so the public buying path stays clean — but they still exist for SEO.
  4. Internal links point back to the right service/guide pages.
  5. Every page’s content is unique.
  6. Listicles / buyer guides live in a buyer’s guide area so they don’t muddy pure thought leadership.
  7. Thought leadership stays in its own area.
  8. People read a lot of it from the blog, while the structure underneath is the service folder system.
  9. Visual/social content is owned by a different person (they said no editor required on that lane).

4. How they produce it (money + people)

  • Q4 example: 150 pieces, about $65,000
  • $500 per piece to employees as side work (not during work hours)
  • $400 per piece to freelancers
  • One strong person owns running the whole strategy
  • They reinvested ad money into people who own a strategy end-to-end

Results they claimed from this system: top 3–5 on almost all target keywords; up to ~$2M/month in bookings; expanded into local content (example: SEO agency in UK) the same way.

6. Video 03 add-ons (same system, more detail)

From the Content Marketing 2026 masterclass — still their system, not ours inventing:

  • IA starts with services + subservices. Vertical pages can rank, but their main job is cognitive fit (“these people do exactly what I need”), not industry spam.
  • B2B macro buckets they use instead of endless industry pages: technology · industrial · services (covers whole B2B TAM).
  • Listicle craft: matching anchor tags in sidebar + body; commercial-intent section (“what to look for in a partner”); TL;DR top picks; AI draft + editorial pass.
  • Guides can rank #1 when internal links are strong; AI-written is OK if quality and links are real.
  • PR / AI overview play: they targeted “best B2B PR agencies,” checked AI overview citations (e.g. Clutch), paid ~$800 to rank #1 on Clutch so AI overview + organic feed each other. Same idea can apply to listicle SERPs we care about.
  • Three pillars of content: Ego (trust us) · Methodology (how we work) · Results (proof).
  • Weekly SEO meeting on the calendar since March 2025 — if the meeting stops, quality slides. Named owner required.
  • Thought leadership links to thought leadership (piece 1 → piece 2) so the cluster compounds.
  • Freelancer pay band they stated: about $350–$500; employees $500 moonlight.
